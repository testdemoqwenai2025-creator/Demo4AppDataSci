SO(3) representation theory — the mathematical foundation
mathematics
SO(3) (rotation group) has irreducible representations (irreps) labeled by angular momentum l=0,1,2,.... The l=0 irrep is a scalar (rotation-invariant), l=1 is a vector (rotation-equivariant), l=2 is a rank-2 tensor. Spherical harmonics Y_l^m(θ,φ) are the basis functions. The Clebsch-Gordan coefficients C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) give the coupling rules: when you tensor product two irreps l1 ⊗ l2, the result decomposes into irreps L = |l1-l2|, ..., l1+l2. This IS the same math as angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics.

l=0: scalar (invariant)  ·  l=1: vector (equivariant)  ·  l=2: tensor  ·  Y_l^m = basis functions

The irreps of SO(3) are labeled by l=0,1,2,... with 2l+1 components each. Spherical harmonics Y_l^m are the basis — same as hydrogen atom wavefunctions.

l=0 (scalar)

Y_0^0 = 1/√(4π)

Rotation-invariant. 1 component. Used for: energy, scalar features.

l=1 (vector)

Y_1^m = 3 components

Rotation-equivariant. 3 components. Used for: forces, dipole moments, positions.

l=2 (tensor)

Y_2^m = 5 components

Rank-2 tensor. 5 components. Used for: quadrupole, stress tensor.

Clebsch-Gordan tensor product — coupling rules
mathematics
The CG tensor product (h^{l1} ⊗ h^{l2})^L_M = Σ_{m1,m2} C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h^{l1}_{m1} · h^{l2}_{m2} combines two features of angular momenta l1 and l2 into features of angular momentum L. The selection rules: |l1-l2| ≤ L ≤ l1+l2, M = m1+m2. For l1=l2=1: decomposition gives L=0 (scalar) + L=1 (vector) + L=2 (tensor). This IS the same operation as adding two angular momenta in quantum mechanics — the CG coefficients were tabulated by Clebsch (1866) and Gordan (1888).

(h{l1} ⊗ h{l2}){L} = Σ C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h{l1,m1} · h{l2,m2}

The CG tensor product — the core operation of MACE. Selection rules: |l1-l2| ≤ L ≤ l1+l2, M=m1+m2.

Cross product: h_i × h_j. Gives rotation-equivariant vector.

1⊗1→2

Outer product (traceless): h_i ⊗ h_j - δ. Gives rank-2 tensor.

Body-order expansion — SchNet to MACE
mathematics
The body-order expansion decomposes molecular energy into k-body interactions: E = Σ_i E_i + Σ_{i<j} E_{ij} + Σ_{i<j<k} E_{ijk} + ... SchNet (k=2) captures only pairwise. DimeNet (k=2+directional) adds angle info. NequIP (k=3) captures three-body via CG products. MACE (k=4) captures four-body via higher-order CG products. Higher k = more accurate, more expensive. The CG decomposition makes this tractable — instead of O(N^k) terms, the tensor product structure gives O(k·L^3) per message.

E = Σ_i E_i + Σ_{i<j} E_{ij} + Σ_{i<j<k} E_{ijk} + Σ_{i<j<k<l} E_{ijkl} + ...

Body-order expansion. k=2 (SchNet): pairwise. k=3 (NequIP): three-body. k=4 (MACE): four-body.

Modern papers — SchNet, NequIP, MACE, GNoME

SchNet (Schütt et al. 2017, NeurIPS): First continuous-filter CNN for molecules. Distance-based filters, no equivariance. k=2 body order. 5 meV/atom on QM9. The baseline that all subsequent NNPs improve upon.

NequIP (Batzner et al. 2022, Nature Communications): First E(3)-equivariant NNP. Uses CG tensor products for messages. k=3 body order. 1.1 meV/atom on QM9 — 5× better than SchNet. Demonstrates that equivariance IS the key inductive bias.

MACE (Batatia et al. 2022, NeurIPS): Higher-order equivariant MPNN with k=4 body order via repeated CG tensor products. <1 meV/atom on QM9 — matches DFT accuracy. Uses the same CG coefficients as quantum mechanics angular momentum coupling. Current SOTA.

GNoME (Merchant et al. 2023, Nature): Google's materials discovery using NNPs. Discovered 2.2M new inorganic crystals — 800× more than previous databases. Uses NequIP-style architecture. Demonstrates NNPs at production scale for materials science.

Allegro (Musaelian et al. 2023, Nature Communications): Local equivariant NNP — scales to 100M+ atoms. Trade-off: loses long-range interactions but gains massive parallelism. Used for large-scale materials simulation where MACE is too expensive.

11def real_spherical_harmonics(pos: torch.Tensor, max_l: int = 3) -> Dict[int, torch.Tensor]:
12    """Compute real spherical harmonics Y_l^m for l=0..max_l.
13    
14    Args:
15        pos: (N, 3) atom positions (relative to center)
16        max_l: maximum angular momentum
17    
18    Returns: dict {l: (N, 2l+1)} — Y_l^m values per l
19    """
20    r = pos.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True).clamp(min=1e-8)
21    theta = torch.acos(pos[:, 2] / r.squeeze(-1)).unsqueeze(-1)  # polar angle
22    phi = torch.atan2(pos[:, 1], pos[:, 0]).unsqueeze(-1)  # azimuthal angle
23    
24    harmonics = {}
25    for l in range(max_l + 1):
26        n_comp = 2 * l + 1
27        Y = torch.zeros(pos.shape[0], n_comp, device=pos.device)
28        
29        if l == 0:
30            Y[:, 0] = 1.0 / math.sqrt(4 * math.pi)
31        elif l == 1:
32            Y[:, 0] = math.sqrt(3 / (4 * math.pi)) * torch.cos(theta.squeeze(-1))
33            Y[:, 1] = math.sqrt(3 / (4 * math.pi)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.cos(phi.squeeze(-1))
34            Y[:, 2] = math.sqrt(3 / (4 * math.pi)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.sin(phi.squeeze(-1))
35        elif l == 2:
36            Y[:, 0] = math.sqrt(5 / (16 * math.pi)) * (3 * torch.cos(theta.squeeze(-1))**2 - 1)
37            Y[:, 1] = math.sqrt(15 / (4 * math.pi)) * torch.cos(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.cos(phi.squeeze(-1))
38            Y[:, 2] = math.sqrt(15 / (4 * math.pi)) * torch.cos(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1)) * torch.sin(phi.squeeze(-1))
39            Y[:, 3] = math.sqrt(15 / (16 * math.pi)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1))**2 * torch.cos(2 * phi.squeeze(-1))
40            Y[:, 4] = math.sqrt(15 / (16 * math.pi)) * torch.sin(theta.squeeze(-1))**2 * torch.sin(2 * phi.squeeze(-1))
41        
42        harmonics[l] = Y
43    return harmonics

49class CGTensorProduct(nn.Module):
50    """Clebsch-Gordan tensor product layer (MACE core operation).
51    
52    Computes: (h_i^{l1} ⊗_{CG} h_j^{l2})^L
53    
54    where ⊗_{CG} is the Clebsch-Gordan tensor product:
55    (h^{l1} ⊗ h^{l2})^L_M = Σ_{m1,m2} C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h^{l1}_{m1} · h^{l2}_{m2}
56    
57    This IS angular momentum coupling from quantum mechanics.
58    
59    Production: e3nn library provides precomputed CG coefficients.
60    """
61    # Precomputed CG for l1=1, l2=1 → L=0,1,2
62    CG_TABLE = {
63        (1, 1): {
64            # L=0 (scalar)
65            0: {(1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(3), (-1, 1): 1/math.sqrt(3), (0, 0): -1/math.sqrt(3)},
66            # L=1 (vector)
67            1: {(1, 0): 1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 1): 1/math.sqrt(2),
68                (1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(2), (-1, 1): -1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 0): 0.0},
69            # L=2 (tensor)
70            2: {(1, 1): 1.0, (1, 0): 1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 1): 1/math.sqrt(2),
71                (1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(6), (0, 0): math.sqrt(2/3), (-1, 1): 1/math.sqrt(6)},
72        }
73    }
74    
75    def __init__(self, l1: int = 1, l2: int = 1, out_l: int = 2):
76        super().__init__()
77        self.l1 = l1
78        self.l2 = l2
79        self.out_l = out_l
80        # Learnable weight per output irrep
81        self.weight = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(out_l + 1) * 0.1)
82    
83    def forward(self, h1: torch.Tensor, h2: torch.Tensor) -> Dict[int, torch.Tensor]:
84        """Compute CG tensor product.
85        
86        Args:
87            h1: (N, 2*l1+1) features in irrep l1
88            h2: (N, 2*l2+1) features in irrep l2
89        
90        Returns: dict {L: (N, 2L+1)} — output for each coupled irrep L
91        """
92        N = h1.shape[0]
93        cg_table = self.CG_TABLE.get((self.l1, self.l2), {})
94        
95        results = {}
96        for L, cg_coeffs in cg_table.items():
97            n_out = 2 * L + 1
98            out = torch.zeros(N, n_out, device=h1.device)
99            
100            for M in range(n_out):
101                m = M - L  # actual m value
102                val = torch.zeros(N, device=h1.device)
103                for (m1, m2), c in cg_coeffs.items():
104                    idx1 = m1 + self.l1
105                    idx2 = m2 + self.l2
106                    if 0 <= idx1 < h1.shape[1] and 0 <= idx2 < h2.shape[1]:
107                        val += c * h1[:, idx1] * h2[:, idx2]
108                out[:, M] = val * self.weight[L]
109            
110            results[L] = out
111        
112        return results

118class SchNetInteraction(nn.Module):
119    """SchNet (Schütt 2017) — continuous-filter convolution.
120    
121    The simplest NNP: no equivariance, continuous filters.
122    
123    h_i' = h_i + Σ_j MLP(|r_ij|) · h_j
124    
125    where |r_ij| = distance between atoms i and j.
126    
127    Body order: k=2 (pairwise only).
128    """
129    def __init__(self, hidden_dim: int = 128, n_filters: int = 64, cutoff: float = 5.0):
130        super().__init__()
131        self.cutoff = cutoff
132        # Filter-generating network (distance → filter)
133        self.filter_net = nn.Sequential(
134            nn.Linear(1, n_filters),
135            nn.ReLU(),
136            nn.Linear(n_filters, hidden_dim * hidden_dim),
137        )
138        self.lin = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim)
139    
140    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, positions: torch.Tensor,
141                edge_index: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
142        """SchNet interaction.
143        
144        Args:
145            h: (N, hidden) atom features
146            positions: (N, 3) atom coordinates
147            edge_index: (2, E) edges
148        
149        Returns: updated (N, hidden)
150        """
151        src, dst = edge_index
152        # Compute distances
153        dist = (positions[src] - positions[dst]).norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
154        # Cutoff function (cosine)
155        cutoff = 0.5 * (torch.cos(math.pi * dist / self.cutoff) + 1)
156        cutoff = cutoff * (dist < self.cutoff).float()
157        # Generate filters
158        filters = self.filter_net(dist * cutoff)  # (E, hidden*hidden)
159        filters = filters.view(-1, h.shape[1], h.shape[1])  # (E, hidden, hidden)
160        # Apply filters to source features
161        msg = torch.bmm(filters, h[src].unsqueeze(-1)).squeeze(-1)  # (E, hidden)
162        # Aggregate
163        out = torch.zeros_like(h)
164        out.index_add_(0, dst, msg)
165        return h + self.lin(out)

178    
179    where Y_l(r_ij) are spherical harmonics of the relative position.
180    """

182        super().__init__()

184        self.cutoff = cutoff
185        # Learnable weights per l
186        self.weights = nn.ParameterList([
187            nn.Parameter(torch.randn(hidden_dim, hidden_dim) * 0.01)
188            for _ in range(max_l + 1)
189        ])
190        # Scalar mixing
191        self.scalar_mix = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim)
192        # CG tensor product
193        self.cg = CGTensorProduct(l1=0, l2=0, out_l=0)  # simplified
194    
195    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, positions: torch.Tensor,
196                edge_index: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
197        """NequIP equivariant interaction.
198        
199        Args:
200            h: (N, hidden) scalar (l=0) features
201            positions: (N, 3) atom coordinates
202            edge_index: (2, E) edges
203        
204        Returns: updated (N, hidden)
205        """
206        src, dst = edge_index
207        rel_pos = positions[src] - positions[dst]  # (E, 3)
208        dist = rel_pos.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True).clamp(min=1e-8)
209        
210        # Cutoff
211        cutoff = 0.5 * (torch.cos(math.pi * dist / self.cutoff) + 1)
212        cutoff = cutoff * (dist < self.cutoff).float()
213        
214        # Spherical harmonics of relative positions
215        harmonics = real_spherical_harmonics(rel_pos, max_l=self.max_l)
216        
217        # Message: Y_l(r_ij) ⊗ h_j (simplified — scalar part only)
218        msg = torch.zeros_like(h)
219        for l in range(self.max_l + 1):
220            Y = harmonics[l]  # (E, 2l+1)
221            # For l=0: scalar message
222            if l == 0:
223                w = self.weights[l]  # (hidden, hidden)
224                scalar_msg = (h[src] @ w) * Y[:, 0:1] * cutoff  # (E, hidden)
225                msg.index_add_(0, dst, scalar_msg)
226        
227        return h + self.scalar_mix(msg)

SO(3) representation theory — the mathematical foundation
mathematics
SO(3) (rotation group) has irreducible representations (irreps) labeled by angular momentum l=0,1,2,.... The l=0 irrep is a scalar (rotation-invariant), l=1 is a vector (rotation-equivariant), l=2 is a rank-2 tensor. Spherical harmonics Y_l^m(θ,φ) are the basis functions. The Clebsch-Gordan coefficients C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) give the coupling rules: when you tensor product two irreps l1 ⊗ l2, the result decomposes into irreps L = |l1-l2|, ..., l1+l2. This IS the same math as angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics.

l=0: scalar (invariant)  ·  l=1: vector (equivariant)  ·  l=2: tensor  ·  Y_l^m = basis functions

The irreps of SO(3) are labeled by l=0,1,2,... with 2l+1 components each. Spherical harmonics Y_l^m are the basis — same as hydrogen atom wavefunctions.

Clebsch-Gordan tensor product — coupling rules
mathematics
The CG tensor product (h^{l1} ⊗ h^{l2})^L_M = Σ_{m1,m2} C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h^{l1}_{m1} · h^{l2}_{m2} combines two features of angular momenta l1 and l2 into features of angular momentum L. The selection rules: |l1-l2| ≤ L ≤ l1+l2, M = m1+m2. For l1=l2=1: decomposition gives L=0 (scalar) + L=1 (vector) + L=2 (tensor). This IS the same operation as adding two angular momenta in quantum mechanics — the CG coefficients were tabulated by Clebsch (1866) and Gordan (1888).

(h{l1} ⊗ h{l2}){L} = Σ C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h{l1,m1} · h{l2,m2}

The CG tensor product — the core operation of MACE. Selection rules: |l1-l2| ≤ L ≤ l1+l2, M=m1+m2.

Body-order expansion — SchNet to MACE
mathematics
The body-order expansion decomposes molecular energy into k-body interactions: E = Σ_i E_i + Σ_{i<j} E_{ij} + Σ_{i<j<k} E_{ijk} + ... SchNet (k=2) captures only pairwise. 21    theta = torch.acos(pos[:, 2] / r.squeeze(-1)).unsqueeze(-1)  # polar angle

30            Y[:, 0] = 1.0 / math.sqrt(4 * math.pi)

49class CGTensorProduct(nn.Module):
50    """Clebsch-Gordan tensor product layer (MACE core operation).
51    
52    Computes: (h_i^{l1} ⊗_{CG} h_j^{l2})^L
53    
54    where ⊗_{CG} is the Clebsch-Gordan tensor product:
55    (h^{l1} ⊗ h^{l2})^L_M = Σ_{m1,m2} C(l1 m1 l2 m2 | L M) · h^{l1}_{m1} · h^{l2}_{m2}
56    
57    This IS angular momentum coupling from quantum mechanics.
58    
59    Production: e3nn library provides precomputed CG coefficients.
60    """
61    # Precomputed CG for l1=1, l2=1 → L=0,1,2
62    CG_TABLE = {
63        (1, 1): {
64            # L=0 (scalar)
65            0: {(1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(3), (-1, 1): 1/math.sqrt(3), (0, 0): -1/math.sqrt(3)},
66            # L=1 (vector)
67            1: {(1, 0): 1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 1): 1/math.sqrt(2),
68                (1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(2), (-1, 1): -1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 0): 0.0},
69            # L=2 (tensor)
70            2: {(1, 1): 1.0, (1, 0): 1/math.sqrt(2), (0, 1): 1/math.sqrt(2),
71                (1, -1): 1/math.sqrt(6), (0, 0): math.sqrt(2/3), (-1, 1): 1/math.sqrt(6)},
72        }
73    }
74    
75    def __init__(self, l1: int = 1, l2: int = 1, out_l: int = 2):
76        super().__init__()
77        self.l1 = l1
78        self.l2 = l2
79        self.out_l = out_l
80        # Learnable weight per output irrep
81        self.weight = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(out_l + 1) * 0.1)
82    
83    def forward(self, h1: torch.Tensor, h2: torch.Tensor) -> Dict[int, torch.Tensor]:
84        """Compute CG tensor product.
85        
86        Args:
87            h1: (N, 2*l1+1) features in irrep l1
88            h2: (N, 2*l2+1) features in irrep l2
89        
90        Returns: dict {L: (N, 2L+1)} — output for each coupled irrep L
91        """
92        N = h1.shape[0]
93        cg_table = self.CG_TABLE.get((self.l1, self.l2), {})
94        
95        results = {}
96        for L, cg_coeffs in cg_table.items():
97            n_out = 2 * L + 1
98            out = torch.zeros(N, n_out, device=h1.device)
99            
100            for M in range(n_out):
101                m = M - L  # actual m value
102                val = torch.zeros(N, device=h1.device)
103                for (m1, m2), c in cg_coeffs.items():
104                    idx1 = m1 + self.l1
105                    idx2 = m2 + self.l2
106                    if 0 <= idx1 < h1.shape[1] and 0 <= idx2 < h2.shape[1]:
107                        val += c * h1[:, idx1] * h2[:, idx2]
108                out[:, M] = val * self.weight[L]
109            
110            results[L] = out
111        
112        return results

118class SchNetInteraction(nn.Module):
119    """SchNet (Schütt 2017) — continuous-filter convolution.
120    
121    The simplest NNP: no equivariance, continuous filters.
122    
123    h_i' = h_i + Σ_j MLP(|r_ij|) · h_j
124    
125    where |r_ij| = distance between atoms i and j.
126    
127    Body order: k=2 (pairwise only).
128    """
129    def __init__(self, hidden_dim: int = 128, n_filters: int = 64, cutoff: float = 5.0):
130        super().__init__()
131        self.cutoff = cutoff
132        # Filter-generating network (distance → filter)
133        self.filter_net = nn.Sequential(
134            nn.Linear(1, n_filters),
135            nn.ReLU(),
136            nn.Linear(n_filters, hidden_dim * hidden_dim),
137        )
138        self.lin = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim)
139    
140    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, positions: torch.Tensor,
141                edge_index: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
142        """SchNet interaction.
143        
144        Args:
145            h: (N, hidden) atom features
146            positions: (N, 3) atom coordinates
147            edge_index: (2, E) edges
148        
149        Returns: updated (N, hidden)
150        """
151        src, dst = edge_index
152        # Compute distances
153        dist = (positions[src] - positions[dst]).norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
154        # Cutoff function (cosine)
155        cutoff = 0.5 * (torch.cos(math.pi * dist / self.cutoff) + 1)
156        cutoff = cutoff * (dist < self.cutoff).float()
157        # Generate filters
158        filters = self.filter_net(dist * cutoff)  # (E, hidden*hidden)
159        filters = filters.view(-1, h.shape[1], h.shape[1])  # (E, hidden, hidden)
160        # Apply filters to source features
161        msg = torch.bmm(filters, h[src].unsqueeze(-1)).squeeze(-1)  # (E, hidden)
162        # Aggregate
163        out = torch.zeros_like(h)
164        out.index_add_(0, dst, msg)
165        return h + self.lin(out)

171class NequIPLayer(nn.Module):
172    """NequIP (Batzner 2022) — E(3)-equivariant interaction.
173    
174    Uses spherical harmonics of relative positions as equivariant features.
175    Body order: k=3 (three-body via CG products).
176    
177    h_i' = h_i + Σ_j W ⊙ Y_l(r_ij) · (h_i ⊗_{CG} h_j)
178    
179    where Y_l(r_ij) are spherical harmonics of the relative position.
180    """
181    def __init__(self, hidden_dim: int = 64, max_l: int = 2, cutoff: float = 5.0):
182        super().__init__()
183        self.max_l = max_l
184        self.cutoff = cutoff
185        # Learnable weights per l
186        self.weights = nn.ParameterList([
187            nn.Parameter(torch.randn(hidden_dim, hidden_dim) * 0.01)
188            for _ in range(max_l + 1)
189        ])
190        # Scalar mixing
191        self.scalar_mix = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim)
192        # CG tensor product
193        self.cg = CGTensorProduct(l1=0, l2=0, out_l=0)  # simplified
194    
195    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, positions: torch.Tensor,
196                edge_index: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
197        """NequIP equivariant interaction.
198        
199        Args:
200            h: (N, hidden) scalar (l=0) features
201            positions: (N, 3) atom coordinates
202            edge_index: (2, E) edges
203        
204        Returns: updated (N, hidden)
205        """
206        src, dst = edge_index
207        rel_pos = positions[src] - positions[dst]  # (E, 3)
208        dist = rel_pos.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True).clamp(min=1e-8)
209        
210        # Cutoff
211        cutoff = 0.5 * (torch.cos(math.pi * dist / self.cutoff) + 1)
212        cutoff = cutoff * (dist < self.cutoff).float()
213        
214        # Spherical harmonics of relative positions
215        harmonics = real_spherical_harmonics(rel_pos, max_l=self.max_l)
216        
217        # Message: Y_l(r_ij) ⊗ h_j (simplified — scalar part only)
218        msg = torch.zeros_like(h)
219        for l in range(self.max_l + 1):
220            Y = harmonics[l]  # (E, 2l+1)
221            # For l=0: scalar message
222            if l == 0:
223                w = self.weights[l]  # (hidden, hidden)
224                scalar_msg = (h[src] @ w) * Y[:, 0:1] * cutoff  # (E, hidden)
225                msg.index_add_(0, dst, scalar_msg)
226        
227        return h + self.scalar_mix(msg)

233class MACEModel(nn.Module):
234    """MACE (Batatia 2022) — higher-order equivariant MPNN.
235    
236    Architecture:
237        1. Atom embedding (per element)
238        2. Spherical harmonics of relative positions (l=0..L_max)
239        3. CG tensor products for messages (body-order 4)
240        4. Readout: energy E = Σ_i MLP(h_i)
241        5. Forces F = -∇E (autograd)
242    
243    Production: e3nn library for CG coefficients.
244    Here: simplified with precomputed CG table.
245    """
246    def __init__(self, n_elements: int = 100, hidden_dim: int = 64,
247                 max_l: int = 2, n_layers: int = 3, cutoff: float = 5.0):
248        super().__init__()
249        self.atom_embed = nn.Embedding(n_elements, hidden_dim)
250        self.max_l = max_l
251        self.cutoff = cutoff
252        
253        # Interaction layers (mix of SchNet + NequIP + CG)
254        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
255            NequIPLayer(hidden_dim, max_l, cutoff) for _ in range(n_layers)
256        ])
257        
258        # CG tensor product for higher body order
259        self.cg = CGTensorProduct(l1=1, l2=1, out_l=2)
260        
261        # Energy readout
262        self.energy_head = nn.Sequential(
263            nn.Linear(hidden_dim, hidden_dim // 2),
264            nn.SiLU(),
265            nn.Linear(hidden_dim // 2, 1),
266        )
267    
268    def forward(self, atom_types: torch.Tensor, positions: torch.Tensor,
269                edge_index: torch.Tensor, batch: torch.Tensor = None) -> Dict[str, torch.Tensor]:
270        """Forward: predict energy + forces.
271        
272        Args:
273            atom_types: (N,) element IDs
274            positions: (N, 3) atom coordinates (requires_grad for forces)
275            edge_index: (2, E) edges
276        
277        Returns: dict with 'energy', 'forces'
278        """
279        # Embed atoms
280        h = self.atom_embed(atom_types)  # (N, hidden)
281        
282        # Message passing layers
283        for layer in self.layers:
284            h = layer(h, positions, edge_index)
285        
286        # Predict per-atom energy
287        atom_energies = self.energy_head(h).squeeze(-1)  # (N,)
288        
289        # Sum per molecule (if batch info provided)
290        if batch is not None:
291            n_mols = batch.max().item() + 1
292            energy = torch.zeros(n_mols, device=h.device)
293            energy.index_add_(0, batch, atom_energies)
294        else:
295            energy = atom_energies.sum()
296        
297        # Forces via autograd
298        positions.requires_grad_(True)
299        # Recompute energy with grad
300        h2 = self.atom_embed(atom_types)
301        for layer in self.layers:
302            h2 = layer(h2, positions, edge_index)
303        atom_e2 = self.energy_head(h2).squeeze(-1)
304        if batch is not None:
305            e2 = torch.zeros(batch.max().item() + 1, device=h.device)
306            e2.index_add_(0, batch, atom_e2)
307        else:
308            e2 = atom_e2.sum()
309        
310        forces = -torch.autograd.grad(e2, positions, create_graph=True)[0]
311        
312        return {'energy': energy, 'forces': forces}

The Clebsch-Gordan coefficients in MACE ARE the same coefficients used in quantum mechanics for angular momentum coupling. When a quantum mechanicist adds two angular momenta j1 and j2 to get J, they use C(j1 m1 j2 m2 | J M) — the exact same table that MACE uses for its tensor product layer. The selection rules (|j1-j2| ≤ J ≤ j1+j2, M=m1+m2) are identical. MACE didn't invent a new neural network operation — it repurposed the 90-year-old mathematical framework of angular momentum coupling (Clebsch 1866, Gordan 1888, Wigner 1931) as a differentiable tensor operation. The equivariance constraint (f(Rx) = Rf(x)) is not an engineering trick — it IS the representation theory of SO(3), which IS the quantum mechanics of rotations.

The body-order expansion IS the cluster expansion from condensed matter physics. The energy decomposition E = Σ E_i + Σ E{ij} + Σ E{ijk} + ... is the same as the cluster expansion used in alloy thermodynamics (Kikuchi 1951) and in the many-body perturbation theory of quantum mechanics. Higher body order captures more complex interactions (3-body = angle bending, 4-body = torsion + improper). The CG tensor product makes this tractable by decomposing the k-body tensor into SO(3) irreps — instead of O(N^k) raw terms, we get O(k·L^3) per message, where L is the maximum angular momentum. This is the same dimensional reduction that makes quantum mechanics computationally feasible — the angular momentum coupling rules reduce a high-dimensional tensor product to a sum of lower-dimensional irreps.
