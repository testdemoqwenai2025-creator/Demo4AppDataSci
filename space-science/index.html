Kepler's laws + orbital mechanics
mathematics
Johannes Kepler (1619) derived three empirical laws from Tycho Brahe's naked-eye observations. Newton (1687) showed they all follow from F = G·M·m/r². Kepler's 3rd law T² = (4π²/GM)·a³ is the foundation of celestial mechanics — it lets you weigh any star from its planet's orbital period alone, and is the same power-law scaling (T ∝ a^1.5) that pervades empirical science (Zipf, Pareto, allometry).

T² = (4π²/GM)·a³  ·  v² = GM·(2/r − 1/a)  ·  F = GMm/r²

Kepler 3rd law (period vs semi-major axis) + vis-viva equation (orbital speed at radius r) + Newton's law of gravitation.

1st law — orbits are ellipses

r(φ) = a(1−e²) / (1 + e·cos φ)

Star at one focus, not the centre. Eccentricity e ∈ [0, 1). Earth's e = 0.0167 (nearly circular).

2nd law — equal areas in equal times

dA/dt = ½ r²(dφ/dt) = const

Planets move faster at perihelion (close approach), slower at aphelion. Conservation of angular momentum.

3rd law — harmonic relation

T² / a³ = 4π² / (GM)

The orbital period squared is proportional to the semi-major axis cubed. Lets you measure M_star from T_planet + a.

Transit method — ΔF/F = (Rp/Rs)²
mathematics
When a planet crosses its host star's disc (transit), it blocks a fraction (Rp/Rs)² of the starlight. Earth-Sun: (R_earth/R_sun)² ≈ 84 ppm — at the Kepler mission's detection limit. Jupiter-Sun: (R_jupiter/R_sun)² ≈ 1% — trivial. Three periodic dips → exoplanet confirmed; the period gives the semi-major axis via Kepler's 3rd law. JWST follow-up spectroscopy during transit reveals atmospheric composition (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄) — transmission spectroscopy.

ΔF/F = (Rp/Rs)²  ·  transit_dur ≈ P·(Rs/a)·(1/π)  ·  a³ = GM·T²/(4π²)

Flux dip = area ratio. Transit duration gives orbital inclination; period gives semi-major axis (Kepler 3rd law).

Detection limit

Earth-Sun: ΔF/F ≈ 84 ppm

Kepler's photometric precision (20 ppm/hr) made Earth-size planet detection around Sun-like stars possible — first in 2009.

Hot Jupiter

HD 209458b: ΔF/F ≈ 1.5%

First transit detection (1999, Charbonneau). Large planet + short period = deep + frequent dips — easiest to find.

Atmospheric follow-up

F(λ) = F₀(λ)·(1 − δ_atm(λ))

JWST transmission spectroscopy: wavelength-dependent transit depth reveals atmospheric absorption bands (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄).

Gravitational wave strain — h = (4G/c⁴)(d²I/dt²)/r
mathematics
General relativity predicts that accelerating mass quadrupoles radiate gravitational waves at the speed of light. The strain h ≈ (4G/c⁴)·(d²I/dt²)/r — for two black holes merging 1.3 billion light-years away (GW150914), the dimensionless strain at Earth was h ≈ 10⁻²¹, meaning a 4-km LIGO arm changed length by ~10⁻¹⁸ m (1/1000 of a proton diameter). LIGO measures this via laser interferometry — the most precise length measurement humanity has ever made.

h ≈ (4G/c⁴)·(d²I/dt²)/r  ·  h_+ = ΔL/L

Quadrupole formula — strain scales as the second time-derivative of the mass quadrupole I_ij, divided by distance r.

Strain amplitude

h ~ 10⁻²¹

For GW150914 at 410 Mpc. A 4-km arm stretches by 4×10⁻¹⁸ m — 1/1000 of a proton's diameter.

Chirp signal

f(ḟ) = (1/π)·(5/256)^(3/8)·(GM_c/c³)^(-5/8)·f^(-11/8)

Frequency rises as BHs inspiral. The chirp mass M_c = (m1·m2)^(3/5)/(m1+m2)^(1/5) is the dominant measurable.

Matched filtering

SNR² = 4·∫|h̃(f)|²/S_n(f) df

Template bank of ~300k chirps. Mathematically equivalent to a single-layer NN with the template as the filter kernel.

LHC jet substructure — n-subjettiness τ_N
mathematics
At the LHC, quarks and gluons produced in 13 TeV proton-proton collisions hadronise into collimated sprays called jets. Jet substructure observables like n-subjettiness discriminate N-prong jets: τ_N = (1/Σp_T)·Σ_k min(p_Tk^β, Σ_i p_Ti^β·(ΔR_ik/R0)^β). For a quark/gluon jet (1-prong), τ_1 ≈ 0; for W→qq' (2-prong), τ_21 = τ_2/τ_1 < 0.45; for top→qqq (3-prong), τ_32 = τ_3/τ_2 < 0.75. Modern taggers replace hand-crafted τ ratios with JetGNNs (ParticleNet) — the same graph-analytics paradigm as systems-biology PPI networks.

τ_N = (1/Σp_{T})·Σ_k min(p_{Tk}^β, Σ_i p_{Ti}^β·(ΔR_{ik}/R0)^β)

N-subjettiness — measures how well the jet's constituents align with N subjets. Smaller τ_N relative to τ_1 means N-prong structure.

Quark/gluon jet (1-prong)

τ_1 ≈ 0, τ_2 ≈ τ_1

Single hard particle carries most p_T. τ_21 ≈ 1 — no substructure. Hardest to tag — relies on radiation patterns.

W/Z jet (2-prong)

τ_21 = τ_2/τ_1 < 0.45

W→qq' produces two prongs. τ_2 small relative to τ_1 — constituents align with 2 subjets.

Top jet (3-prong)

τ_32 = τ_3/τ_2 < 0.75

t→bW→bqq' produces three prongs. τ_3 small relative to τ_2 — constituents align with 3 subjets.

19class LightCurveAugmentation(nn.Module):
20    """Augment light curves to simulate stellar variability + detector noise.
21    
22    Real Kepler light curves contain:
23        - Stellar variability (sinusoidal on hours-days timescales)
24        - Detector drift (systematics, ~1 ppm/quarter)
25        - Photon noise (~sqrt(N_photon))
26    
27    Augment: add Gaussian noise + random sinusoid + cosmic-ray spikes.
28    """
29    def __init__(self, noise_ppm: int = 200):
30        super().__init__()
31        self.noise_ppm = noise_ppm
32    
33    def forward(self, flux: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
34        # flux shape: (B, L) — in flux units (1.0 = no transit)
35        B, L = flux.shape
36        noise = torch.randn_like(flux) * (self.noise_ppm * 1e-6)
37        # Stellar variability (slow sinusoid)
38        t = torch.arange(L, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) / L
39        amp = torch.rand(B, 1, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) * 200e-6
40        freq = torch.rand(B, 1, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) * 5 + 1
41        sinusoid = amp * torch.sin(2 * math.pi * freq * t.unsqueeze(0))
42        return flux + noise + sinusoid

44class TransitCNN(nn.Module):
45    """1D CNN to detect exoplanet transits in light curves.
46    
47    Input:  (B, L) flux time series, L = 2048 cadences (~28 days @ 30-min)
48    Output: (B, 2) logits  [no transit, transit]
49    
50    Architecture: 4 conv blocks with BatchNorm + ReLU + MaxPool, then global
51    avg pool + linear head. Total params ~50k — small enough for inference
52    on a single GPU.
53    """
54    def __init__(self, length: int = 2048, channels: int = 32, num_classes: int = 2):
55        super().__init__()
56        self.length = length
57        self.features = nn.Sequential(
58            self._block(1,         channels,    kernel=7, pool=4),  # 2048 → 512
59            self._block(channels,  channels*2,  kernel=5, pool=4),  # 512  → 128
60            self._block(channels*2,channels*4,  kernel=5, pool=4),  # 128  → 32
61            self._block(channels*4,channels*8,  kernel=3, pool=2),  # 32   → 16
62        )
63        self.global_pool = nn.AdaptiveAvgPool1d(1)
64        self.head = nn.Sequential(
65            nn.Flatten(),
66            nn.Linear(channels * 8, 64),
67            nn.ReLU(),
68            nn.Dropout(0.3),
69            nn.Linear(64, num_classes),
70        )
71    
72    @staticmethod
73    def _block(in_c: int, out_c: int, kernel: int, pool: int) -> nn.Sequential:
74        return nn.Sequential(
75            nn.Conv1d(in_c, out_c, kernel_size=kernel, padding=kernel // 2),
76            nn.BatchNorm1d(out_c),
77            nn.ReLU(),
78            nn.MaxPool1d(pool),
79        )
80    
81    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
82        # x: (B, L) → (B, 1, L)
83        x = x.unsqueeze(1)
84        x = self.features(x)               # (B, C*8, L')
85        x = self.global_pool(x)            # (B, C*8, 1)
86        return self.head(x)                # (B, num_classes)

108class QTransform(nn.Module):
109    """Differentiable Q-transform (constant-Q time-frequency transform).
110    
111    LIGO uses Morlet wavelets in production (gwpy / cuQwavelet on GPU).
112    Here we approximate as STFT + log-frequency interpolation — same shape
113    out, simpler to implement in pure PyTorch.
114    """
115    def __init__(self, n_freq: int = 64, n_time: int = 64, sample_rate: int = 4096):
116        super().__init__()
117        self.n_freq = n_freq
118        self.n_time = n_time
119        self.sr = sample_rate
120        # Constant-Q log-spaced frequency bins (20 Hz to 1024 Hz — LIGO band)
121        f_min, f_max = 20, 1024
122        freqs = torch.logspace(math.log10(f_min), math.log10(f_max), n_freq)
123        self.register_buffer('freqs', freqs)
124    
125    def forward(self, strain: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
126        # strain: (B, T) at sample_rate Hz
127        B, T = strain.shape
128        # STFT with 256-pt Hann window
129        window = torch.hann_window(256, device=strain.device, dtype=strain.dtype)
130        spec = torch.stft(strain, n_fft=256, hop_length=max(1, T // self.n_time),
131                          win_length=256, window=window, return_complex=True)
132        spec = spec.abs()  # (B, F, T')
133        # Interpolate to fixed (F, T) shape
134        spec = F.interpolate(spec.unsqueeze(1), size=(self.n_freq, self.n_time),
135                            mode='bilinear', align_corners=False)
136        return spec  # (B, 1, F, T)

145    ~1M params — lightweight enough for online inference at LHO/LLO.

147    """
148    def __init__(self, n_freq: int = 64, n_time: int = 64, num_classes: int = 4):
149        super().__init__()
150        self.q_transform = QTransform(n_freq, n_time)
151        c = 16
152        self.backbone = nn.Sequential(
153            self._conv_block(1,    c,    k=3),
154            self._conv_block(c,    c*2,  k=3),
155            self._conv_block(c*2,  c*4,  k=3),
156            self._conv_block(c*4,  c*8,  k=3),
157            self._conv_block(c*8,  c*16, k=3),
158        )
159        self.global_pool = nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1)
160        self.head = nn.Sequential(
161            nn.Flatten(),
162            nn.Linear(c*16, 128),
163            nn.ReLU(),
164            nn.Dropout(0.4),
165            nn.Linear(128, num_classes),
166        )
167    
168    @staticmethod
169    def _conv_block(in_c: int, out_c: int, k: int) -> nn.Sequential:
170        return nn.Sequential(
171            nn.Conv2d(in_c, out_c, k, padding=k // 2),
172            nn.BatchNorm2d(out_c),
173            nn.ReLU(),
174            nn.MaxPool2d(2),
175        )
176    
177    def forward(self, strain: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
178        spec = self.q_transform(strain)        # (B, 1, F, T)
179        x = self.backbone(spec)                # (B, C*16, F', T')
180        x = self.global_pool(x)               # (B, C*16, 1, 1)
181        return self.head(x)                    # (B, num_classes)
182    
183    @staticmethod
184    def synthesize_chirp(B: int = 1, T: int = 8192, sr: int = 4096,
185                          f_start: float = 30.0, f_end: float = 250.0) -> torch.Tensor:
186        """Build a synthetic BBH chirp for testing (f increases with t)."""
187        t = torch.linspace(0, T / sr, T).unsqueeze(0)  # (1, T)
188        # Linear chirp (real inspiral is power-law, but linear is enough for tests)
189        f = f_start + (f_end - f_start) * t
190        phase = 2 * math.pi * torch.cumsum(f, dim=-1) / sr
191        amp = t / (T / sr)  # amplitude grows linearly (very rough approx)
192        return 1e-21 * amp * torch.sin(phase).expand(B, -1).contiguous()

202class ParticleEmbedding(nn.Module):
203    """Embed each particle's 4-momentum into a feature vector.
204    
205    Features per particle:
206        log(pT), log(E), η (pseudorapidity), φ (azimuthal angle)
207        + pdg_id (embedded, particle type)
208    """
209    def __init__(self, d_model: int = 64, n_pdg: int = 50):
210        super().__init__()
211        self.linear = nn.Linear(4, d_model)
212        self.pdg_embed = nn.Embedding(n_pdg, d_model)
213        self.combine = nn.Linear(d_model * 2, d_model)
214    
215    def forward(self, pT: torch.Tensor, eta: torch.Tensor, phi: torch.Tensor,
216                E: torch.Tensor, pdg_id: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
217        # All inputs: (B, N)
218        kin = torch.stack([torch.log(pT + 1), torch.log(E + 1), eta, phi], dim=-1)  # (B, N, 4)
219        h_kin = self.linear(kin)              # (B, N, d)
220        h_pdg = self.pdg_embed(pdg_id)         # (B, N, d)
221        h = torch.cat([h_kin, h_pdg], dim=-1)  # (B, N, 2d)
222        return F.relu(self.combine(h))         # (B, N, d)
223 
224class EdgeConvLayer(nn.Module):
225    """EdgeConv layer — graph conv with messages as functions of edge features.
226    
227    For each node i and neighbor j:
228        e_ij = h_i || (h_j - h_i)              (pairwise difference)
229        m_ij = MLP(e_ij)                       (edge message)
230        h_i' = max_k(m_ik for k in N(i))        (permutation-invariant aggregation)
231    
232    kNN graph built in ΔR = sqrt(Δη² + Δφ²) space — recomputed at each layer.
233    
234    Reference: Dynamic Graph CNN (Wang et al. 2019) → ParticleNet (Qu & Gouskos 2020).
235    """
236    def __init__(self, d_in: int, d_out: int, k: int = 8):
237        super().__init__()
238        self.k = k
239        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
240            nn.Linear(d_in * 2, d_out),
241            nn.ReLU(),
242            nn.Linear(d_out, d_out),
243        )
244    
245    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, coords: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
246        # h: (B, N, d_in), coords: (B, N, 2)  (η, φ)
247        B, N, D = h.shape
248        # Pairwise ΔR = sqrt(Δη² + Δφ²)
249        diff = coords.unsqueeze(2) - coords.unsqueeze(1)    # (B, N, N, 2)
250        dR = torch.sqrt((diff ** 2).sum(-1))                # (B, N, N)
251        # kNN: k nearest neighbors (smallest ΔR)
252        _, idx = torch.topk(dR, self.k, dim=-1, largest=False)  # (B, N, k)
253        # Gather neighbor features
254        idx_exp = idx.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1, -1, -1, D)   # (B, N, k, D)
255        h_neighbors = torch.gather(
256            h.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, N, -1, -1), 2, idx_exp
257        )                                                   # (B, N, k, D)
258        # Edge feature:  h_i || (h_j - h_i)
259        h_i = h.unsqueeze(2).expand(-1, -1, self.k, -1)     # (B, N, k, D)
260        edge = torch.cat([h_i, h_neighbors - h_i], dim=-1)  # (B, N, k, 2D)
261        # MLP on edges
262        m = self.mlp(edge)                                  # (B, N, k, d_out)
263        # Aggregate via max (permutation-invariant)
264        h_new, _ = m.max(dim=2)                             # (B, N, d_out)
265        return h_new, dR
266 
267class JetGNN(nn.Module):
268    """Graph neural network for jet classification (quark/gluon/W/top).
269    
270    Particles = nodes, ΔR = edges.  Stack of EdgeConv layers (recompute kNN at
271    each layer) + global mean pool + linear head.
272    
273    Input (variable-length particle sets):
274        pT:     (B, N)  transverse momenta (GeV)
275        eta:    (B, N)  pseudorapidities
276        phi:    (B, N)  azimuthal angles
277        E:      (B, N)  energies (GeV)
278        pdg_id: (B, N)  particle-type IDs (long)
279    Output:
280        logits: (B, 4)  [quark, gluon, W-boson, top-quark]
281    
282    Reference: ParticleNet (Qu & Gouskos 2020) — standard jet-GNN at the LHC.
283    """
284    def __init__(self, d_model: int = 64, n_layers: int = 3, k: int = 8,
285                 num_classes: int = 4, n_pdg: int = 50):
286        super().__init__()
287        self.embed = ParticleEmbedding(d_model, n_pdg)
288        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
289            EdgeConvLayer(d_model, d_model, k) for _ in range(n_layers)
290        ])
291        # Focal loss could be used for class imbalance (gluon vs top) — omitted for clarity
292        self.head = nn.Sequential(
293            nn.Linear(d_model, d_model),
294            nn.ReLU(),
295            nn.Dropout(0.3),
296            nn.Linear(d_model, num_classes),
297        )
298    
299    def forward(self, pT: torch.Tensor, eta: torch.Tensor, phi: torch.Tensor,
300                E: torch.Tensor, pdg_id: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
301        h = self.embed(pT, eta, phi, E, pdg_id)             # (B, N, d)
302        coords = torch.stack([eta, phi], dim=-1)            # (B, N, 2)
303        for layer in self.layers:
304            h_new, _ = layer(h, coords)
305            h = h + h_new                                    # residual
306        h = h.mean(dim=1)                                    # (B, d) — global mean pool
307        return self.head(h)                                  # (B, num_classes)

Modern papers — Kepler, TESS, LIGO, LHC, JWST

Kepler Mission (Borucki et al. 2010, ApJL 713:L126): The Kepler space telescope (2009–2018) monitored 150,000 stars for transits, discovering 2,778 confirmed exoplanets and ~4,000 candidates. Demonstrated that Earth-size planets around Sun-like stars are common (~10% occurrence rate). The mission's photometric precision (20 ppm/hr on a 12th-magnitude star) enabled the first rocky-planet detections. The Kepler Input Catalogue and TESS Input Catalogue together index 1.7 billion stars — the most extensive stellar database ever compiled.

TESS Mission (Ricker et al. 2015, JATIS 1:014003): The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (2018–) surveys the entire sky in 26-sectored 27-day pointings, focusing on bright (V < 12) stars for follow-up characterisation. TESS has discovered 400+ confirmed exoplanets and 6,000+ candidates (TOIs). The mission produces ~200M light curves in its prime survey — each ~30,000 cadences of 2-min or 20-sec integrations — all stored in the MAST archive as Parquet + FITS files.

LIGO GW150914 (Abbott et al. 2016, PRL 116:061102): First direct detection of gravitational waves — the inspiral and merger of two black holes (36 + 29 solar masses) 410 Mpc away. The signal, observed on 14 September 2015, swept through LIGO's two 4-km interferometers (Hanford + Livingston) with a 6.9 ms delay matching light-speed travel between sites. Strain amplitude h ≈ 10⁻²¹ — the most precise length measurement ever made. By the end of O3 (2020), LIGO/Virgo had catalogued 90 GW events (50 BBH, 2 BNS, 1 NSBH, 37 marginal).

LHC Higgs Boson (ATLAS + CMS, 2012, PLB 716:1–29): Discovery of the Higgs boson at m_H ≈ 125 GeV, confirming the Standard Model's last unverified prediction (Higgs 1964). The LHC produces 40 million collisions per second at 13 TeV — only ~1000 events/s survive the trigger. After zero-suppression, ATLAS + CMS together record ~1 PB/year. Jet substructure algorithms (anti-kT, n-subjettiness, JetGNNs) identify boosted H→bb̄, W→qq', top→bqq' — the LHC's biggest machine-learning pipeline.

JWST Early Galaxies (Naidu et al. 2022, ApJL): JWST's CEERS survey revealed galaxy candidates at z ≈ 10–17 (within 300 million years of the Big Bang) — far earlier than Hubble could see. The photometric redshift technique (Lyman-break dropout) plus NIRSpec follow-up spectroscopy confirmed Lyman-α emission. JWST produces ~10 TB/day of raw imaging — Cal pipeline outputs are stored at MAST as Parquet/Arrow tables for community querying. Early-galaxy observations challenge Λ-CDM cosmology — some galaxies appear too massive too soon.

Kepler's laws + orbital mechanics
mathematics
Johannes Kepler (1619) derived three empirical laws from Tycho Brahe's naked-eye observations. Newton (1687) showed they all follow from F = G·M·m/r². Kepler's 3rd law T² = (4π²/GM)·a³ is the foundation of celestial mechanics — it lets you weigh any star from its planet's orbital period alone, and is the same power-law scaling (T ∝ a^1.5) that pervades empirical science (Zipf, Pareto, allometry).

T² = (4π²/GM)·a³  ·  v² = GM·(2/r − 1/a)  ·  F = GMm/r²

Kepler 3rd law (period vs semi-major axis) + vis-viva equation (orbital speed at radius r) + Newton's law of gravitation.

1st law — orbits are ellipses

r(φ) = a(1−e²) / (1 + e·cos φ)

Star at one focus, not the centre. Eccentricity e ∈ [0, 1). Earth's e = 0.0167 (nearly circular).

2nd law — equal areas in equal times

dA/dt = ½ r²(dφ/dt) = const

Planets move faster at perihelion (close approach), slower at aphelion. Conservation of angular momentum.

3rd law — harmonic relation

T² / a³ = 4π² / (GM)

The orbital period squared is proportional to the semi-major axis cubed. Lets you measure M_star from T_planet + a.

Transit method — ΔF/F = (Rp/Rs)²
mathematics
When a planet crosses its host star's disc (transit), it blocks a fraction (Rp/Rs)² of the starlight. Earth-Sun: (R_earth/R_sun)² ≈ 84 ppm — at the Kepler mission's detection limit. Jupiter-Sun: (R_jupiter/R_sun)² ≈ 1% — trivial. Three periodic dips → exoplanet confirmed; the period gives the semi-major axis via Kepler's 3rd law. JWST follow-up spectroscopy during transit reveals atmospheric composition (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄) — transmission spectroscopy.

ΔF/F = (Rp/Rs)²  ·  transit_dur ≈ P·(Rs/a)·(1/π)  ·  a³ = GM·T²/(4π²)

Flux dip = area ratio. Transit duration gives orbital inclination; period gives semi-major axis (Kepler 3rd law).

Detection limit

Earth-Sun: ΔF/F ≈ 84 ppm

Kepler's photometric precision (20 ppm/hr) made Earth-size planet detection around Sun-like stars possible — first in 2009.

Hot Jupiter

HD 209458b: ΔF/F ≈ 1.5%

First transit detection (1999, Charbonneau). Large planet + short period = deep + frequent dips — easiest to find.

Atmospheric follow-up

F(λ) = F₀(λ)·(1 − δ_atm(λ))

JWST transmission spectroscopy: wavelength-dependent transit depth reveals atmospheric absorption bands (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄).

Gravitational wave strain — h = (4G/c⁴)(d²I/dt²)/r
mathematics
General relativity predicts that accelerating mass quadrupoles radiate gravitational waves at the speed of light. The strain h ≈ (4G/c⁴)·(d²I/dt²)/r — for two black holes merging 1.3 billion light-years away (GW150914), the dimensionless strain at Earth was h ≈ 10⁻²¹, meaning a 4-km LIGO arm changed length by ~10⁻¹⁸ m (1/1000 of a proton diameter). LIGO measures this via laser interferometry — the most precise length measurement humanity has ever made.

h ≈ (4G/c⁴)·(d²I/dt²)/r  ·  h_+ = ΔL/L

Quadrupole formula — strain scales as the second time-derivative of the mass quadrupole I_ij, divided by distance r.

Strain amplitude

h ~ 10⁻²¹

For GW150914 at 410 Mpc. A 4-km arm stretches by 4×10⁻¹⁸ m — 1/1000 of a proton's diameter.

Chirp signal

f(ḟ) = (1/π)·(5/256)^(3/8)·(GM_c/c³)^(-5/8)·f^(-11/8)

Frequency rises as BHs inspiral. The chirp mass M_c = (m1·m2)^(3/5)/(m1+m2)^(1/5) is the dominant measurable.

Matched filtering

SNR² = 4·∫|h̃(f)|²/S_n(f) df

Template bank of ~300k chirps. Mathematically equivalent to a single-layer NN with the template as the filter kernel.

LHC jet substructure — n-subjettiness τ_N
mathematics
At the LHC, quarks and gluons produced in 13 TeV proton-proton collisions hadronise into collimated sprays called jets. Jet substructure observables like n-subjettiness discriminate N-prong jets: τ_N = (1/Σp_T)·Σ_k min(p_Tk^β, Σ_i p_Ti^β·(ΔR_ik/R0)^β). For a quark/gluon jet (1-prong), τ_1 ≈ 0; for W→qq' (2-prong), τ_21 = τ_2/τ_1 < 0.45; for top→qqq (3-prong), τ_32 = τ_3/τ_2 < 0.75. Modern taggers replace hand-crafted τ ratios with JetGNNs (ParticleNet) — the same graph-analytics paradigm as systems-biology PPI networks.

τ_N = (1/Σp_{T})·Σ_k min(p_{Tk}^β, Σ_i p_{Ti}^β·(ΔR_{ik}/R0)^β)

N-subjettiness — measures how well the jet's constituents align with N subjets. Smaller τ_N relative to τ_1 means N-prong structure.

Quark/gluon jet (1-prong)

τ_1 ≈ 0, τ_2 ≈ τ_1

Single hard particle carries most p_T. τ_21 ≈ 1 — no substructure. Hardest to tag — relies on radiation patterns.

W/Z jet (2-prong)

τ_21 = τ_2/τ_1 < 0.45

W→qq' produces two prongs. τ_2 small relative to τ_1 — constituents align with 2 subjets.

Top jet (3-prong)

τ_32 = τ_3/τ_2 < 0.75

t→bW→bqq' produces three prongs. τ_3 small relative to τ_2 — constituents align with 3 subjets.

19class LightCurveAugmentation(nn.Module):
20    """Augment light curves to simulate stellar variability + detector noise.
21    
22    Real Kepler light curves contain:
23        - Stellar variability (sinusoidal on hours-days timescales)
24        - Detector drift (systematics, ~1 ppm/quarter)
25        - Photon noise (~sqrt(N_photon))
26    
27    Augment: add Gaussian noise + random sinusoid + cosmic-ray spikes.
28    """
29    def __init__(self, noise_ppm: int = 200):
30        super().__init__()
31        self.noise_ppm = noise_ppm
32    
33    def forward(self, flux: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
34        # flux shape: (B, L) — in flux units (1.0 = no transit)
35        B, L = flux.shape
36        noise = torch.randn_like(flux) * (self.noise_ppm * 1e-6)
37        # Stellar variability (slow sinusoid)
38        t = torch.arange(L, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) / L
39        amp = torch.rand(B, 1, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) * 200e-6
40        freq = torch.rand(B, 1, device=flux.device, dtype=flux.dtype) * 5 + 1
41        sinusoid = amp * torch.sin(2 * math.pi * freq * t.unsqueeze(0))
42        return flux + noise + sinusoid

44class TransitCNN(nn.Module):
45    """1D CNN to detect exoplanet transits in light curves.
46    
47    Input:  (B, L) flux time series, L = 2048 cadences (~28 days @ 30-min)
48    Output: (B, 2) logits  [no transit, transit]
49    
50    Architecture: 4 conv blocks with BatchNorm + ReLU + MaxPool, then global
51    avg pool + linear head. Total params ~50k — small enough for inference
52    on a single GPU.
53    """
54    def __init__(self, length: int = 2048, channels: int = 32, num_classes: int = 2):
55        super().__init__()
56        self.length = length
57        self.features = nn.Sequential(
58            self._block(1,         channels,    kernel=7, pool=4),  # 2048 → 512
59            self._block(channels,  channels*2,  kernel=5, pool=4),  # 512  → 128
60            self._block(channels*2,channels*4,  kernel=5, pool=4),  # 128  → 32
61            self._block(channels*4,channels*8,  kernel=3, pool=2),  # 32   → 16
62        )
63        self.global_pool = nn.AdaptiveAvgPool1d(1)
64        self.head = nn.Sequential(
65            nn.Flatten(),
66            nn.Linear(channels * 8, 64),
67            nn.ReLU(),
68            nn.Dropout(0.3),
69            nn.Linear(64, num_classes),
70        )
71    
72    @staticmethod
73    def _block(in_c: int, out_c: int, kernel: int, pool: int) -> nn.Sequential:
74        return nn.Sequential(
75            nn.Conv1d(in_c, out_c, kernel_size=kernel, padding=kernel // 2),
76            nn.BatchNorm1d(out_c),
77            nn.ReLU(),
78            nn.MaxPool1d(pool),
79        )
80    
81    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
82        # x: (B, L) → (B, 1, L)
83        x = x.unsqueeze(1)
84        x = self.features(x)               # (B, C*8, L')
85        x = self.global_pool(x)            # (B, C*8, 1)
86        return self.head(x)                # (B, num_classes)

102# 2. GravitationalWaveClassifier — 2D CNN on Q-transform spectrograms
103# ============================================================
104# LIGO strain data is 1D at 4096 Hz. Convert to 2D time-frequency
105# representation via Q-transform (constant-Q — better than STFT for chirps).
106# Each event: ~2 sec window → 64x64 spectrogram. 2D CNN detects the chirp.

108class QTransform(nn.Module):
109    """Differentiable Q-transform (constant-Q time-frequency transform).
110    
111    LIGO uses Morlet wavelets in production (gwpy / cuQwavelet on GPU).
112    Here we approximate as STFT + log-frequency interpolation — same shape
113    out, simpler to implement in pure PyTorch.
114    """
115    def __init__(self, n_freq: int = 64, n_time: int = 64, sample_rate: int = 4096):
116        super().__init__()
117        self.n_freq = n_freq
118        self.n_time = n_time
119        self.sr = sample_rate
120        # Constant-Q log-spaced frequency bins (20 Hz to 1024 Hz — LIGO band)
121        f_min, f_max = 20, 1024
122        freqs = torch.logspace(math.log10(f_min), math.log10(f_max), n_freq)
123        self.register_buffer('freqs', freqs)
124    
125    def forward(self, strain: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
126        # strain: (B, T) at sample_rate Hz
127        B, T = strain.shape
128        # STFT with 256-pt Hann window
129        window = torch.hann_window(256, device=strain.device, dtype=strain.dtype)
130        spec = torch.stft(strain, n_fft=256, hop_length=max(1, T // self.n_time),
131                          win_length=256, window=window, return_complex=True)
132        spec = spec.abs()  # (B, F, T')
133        # Interpolate to fixed (F, T) shape
134        spec = F.interpolate(spec.unsqueeze(1), size=(self.n_freq, self.n_time),
135                            mode='bilinear', align_corners=False)
136        return spec  # (B, 1, F, T)

138class GravitationalWaveClassifier(nn.Module):
139    """2D CNN to classify GW events: BBH / BNS / NSBH / Noise.
140    
141    Input:  (B, T) strain timeseries (T ~ 8192 samples at 4096 Hz = 2 s)
142    Output: (B, 4) logits
143    
144    Architecture: 5 conv blocks (3x3 kernels) + global avg pool + linear head.
145    ~1M params — lightweight enough for online inference at LHO/LLO.
146    Inspired by Gravity Spy (Zevin et al. 2017) + DeepCW (George & Huerta 2018).
147    """
148    def __init__(self, n_freq: int = 64, n_time: int = 64, num_classes: int = 4):
149        super().__init__()
150        self.q_transform = QTransform(n_freq, n_time)
151        c = 16
152        self.backbone = nn.Sequential(
153            self._conv_block(1,    c,    k=3),
154            self._conv_block(c,    c*2,  k=3),
155            self._conv_block(c*2,  c*4,  k=3),
156            self._conv_block(c*4,  c*8,  k=3),
157            self._conv_block(c*8,  c*16, k=3),
158        )
159        self.global_pool = nn.AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1)
160        self.head = nn.Sequential(
161            nn.Flatten(),
162            nn.Linear(c*16, 128),
163            nn.ReLU(),
164            nn.Dropout(0.4),
165            nn.Linear(128, num_classes),
166        )
167    
168    @staticmethod
169    def _conv_block(in_c: int, out_c: int, k: int) -> nn.Sequential:
170        return nn.Sequential(
171            nn.Conv2d(in_c, out_c, k, padding=k // 2),
172            nn.BatchNorm2d(out_c),
173            nn.ReLU(),
174            nn.MaxPool2d(2),
175        )
176    
177    def forward(self, strain: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
178        spec = self.q_transform(strain)        # (B, 1, F, T)
179        x = self.backbone(spec)                # (B, C*16, F', T')
180        x = self.global_pool(x)               # (B, C*16, 1, 1)
181        return self.head(x)                    # (B, num_classes)

183    @staticmethod
184    def synthesize_chirp(B: int = 1, T: int = 8192, sr: int = 4096,
185                          f_start: float = 30.0, f_end: float = 250.0) -> torch.Tensor:
186        """Build a synthetic BBH chirp for testing (f increases with t)."""
187        t = torch.linspace(0, T / sr, T).unsqueeze(0)  # (1, T)
188        # Linear chirp (real inspiral is power-law, but linear is enough for tests)
189        f = f_start + (f_end - f_start) * t
190        phase = 2 * math.pi * torch.cumsum(f, dim=-1) / sr
191        amp = t / (T / sr)  # amplitude grows linearly (very rough approx)
192        return 1e-21 * amp * torch.sin(phase).expand(B, -1).contiguous()

202class ParticleEmbedding(nn.Module):
203    """Embed each particle's 4-momentum into a feature vector.
204    
205    Features per particle:
206        log(pT), log(E), η (pseudorapidity), φ (azimuthal angle)
207        + pdg_id (embedded, particle type)
208    """
209    def __init__(self, d_model: int = 64, n_pdg: int = 50):
210        super().__init__()
211        self.linear = nn.Linear(4, d_model)
212        self.pdg_embed = nn.Embedding(n_pdg, d_model)
213        self.combine = nn.Linear(d_model * 2, d_model)
214    
215    def forward(self, pT: torch.Tensor, eta: torch.Tensor, phi: torch.Tensor,
216                E: torch.Tensor, pdg_id: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
217        # All inputs: (B, N)
218        kin = torch.stack([torch.log(pT + 1), torch.log(E + 1), eta, phi], dim=-1)  # (B, N, 4)
219        h_kin = self.linear(kin)              # (B, N, d)
220        h_pdg = self.pdg_embed(pdg_id)         # (B, N, d)
221        h = torch.cat([h_kin, h_pdg], dim=-1)  # (B, N, 2d)
222        return F.relu(self.combine(h))         # (B, N, d)

224class EdgeConvLayer(nn.Module):
225    """EdgeConv layer — graph conv with messages as functions of edge features.
226    
227    For each node i and neighbor j:
228        e_ij = h_i || (h_j - h_i)              (pairwise difference)
229        m_ij = MLP(e_ij)                       (edge message)
230        h_i' = max_k(m_ik for k in N(i))        (permutation-invariant aggregation)
231    
232    kNN graph built in ΔR = sqrt(Δη² + Δφ²) space — recomputed at each layer.
233    
234    Reference: Dynamic Graph CNN (Wang et al. 2019) → ParticleNet (Qu & Gouskos 2020).
235    """
236    def __init__(self, d_in: int, d_out: int, k: int = 8):
237        super().__init__()
238        self.k = k
239        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
240            nn.Linear(d_in * 2, d_out),
241            nn.ReLU(),
242            nn.Linear(d_out, d_out),
243        )
244    
245    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor, coords: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
246        # h: (B, N, d_in), coords: (B, N, 2)  (η, φ)
247        B, N, D = h.shape
248        # Pairwise ΔR = sqrt(Δη² + Δφ²)
249        diff = coords.unsqueeze(2) - coords.unsqueeze(1)    # (B, N, N, 2)
250        dR = torch.sqrt((diff ** 2).sum(-1))                # (B, N, N)
251        # kNN: k nearest neighbors (smallest ΔR)
252        _, idx = torch.topk(dR, self.k, dim=-1, largest=False)  # (B, N, k)
253        # Gather neighbor features
254        idx_exp = idx.unsqueeze(-1).expand(-1, -1, -1, D)   # (B, N, k, D)
255        h_neighbors = torch.gather(
256            h.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, N, -1, -1), 2, idx_exp
257        )                                                   # (B, N, k, D)
258        # Edge feature:  h_i || (h_j - h_i)
259        h_i = h.unsqueeze(2).expand(-1, -1, self.k, -1)     # (B, N, k, D)
260        edge = torch.cat([h_i, h_neighbors - h_i], dim=-1)  # (B, N, k, 2D)
261        # MLP on edges
262        m = self.mlp(edge)                                  # (B, N, k, d_out)
263        # Aggregate via max (permutation-invariant)
264        h_new, _ = m.max(dim=2)                             # (B, N, d_out)
265        return h_new, dR

267class JetGNN(nn.Module):
268    """Graph neural network for jet classification (quark/gluon/W/top).
269    
270    Particles = nodes, ΔR = edges.  Stack of EdgeConv layers (recompute kNN at
271    each layer) + global mean pool + linear head.
272    
273    Input (variable-length particle sets):
274        pT:     (B, N)  transverse momenta (GeV)
275        eta:    (B, N)  pseudorapidities
276        phi:    (B, N)  azimuthal angles
277        E:      (B, N)  energies (GeV)
278        pdg_id: (B, N)  particle-type IDs (long)
279    Output:
280        logits: (B, 4)  [quark, gluon, W-boson, top-quark]
281    
282    Reference: ParticleNet (Qu & Gouskos 2020) — standard jet-GNN at the LHC.
283    """
284    def __init__(self, d_model: int = 64, n_layers: int = 3, k: int = 8,
285                 num_classes: int = 4, n_pdg: int = 50):
286        super().__init__()
287        self.embed = ParticleEmbedding(d_model, n_pdg)
288        self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
289            EdgeConvLayer(d_model, d_model, k) for _ in range(n_layers)
290        ])
291        # Focal loss could be used for class imbalance (gluon vs top) — omitted for clarity
292        self.head = nn.Sequential(
293            nn.Linear(d_model, d_model),
294            nn.ReLU(),
295            nn.Dropout(0.3),
296            nn.Linear(d_model, num_classes),
297        )
298    
299    def forward(self, pT: torch.Tensor, eta: torch.Tensor, phi: torch.Tensor,
300                E: torch.Tensor, pdg_id: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
301        h = self.embed(pT, eta, phi, E, pdg_id)             # (B, N, d)
302        coords = torch.stack([eta, phi], dim=-1)            # (B, N, 2)
303        for layer in self.layers:
304            h_new, _ = layer(h, coords)
305            h = h + h_new                                    # residual
306        h = h.mean(dim=1)                                    # (B, d) — global mean pool
307        return self.head(h)                                  # (B, num_classes)

Modern papers — Kepler, TESS, LIGO, LHC, JWST

Kepler Mission (Borucki et al. 2010, ApJL 713:L126): The Kepler space telescope (2009–2018) monitored 150,000 stars for transits, discovering 2,778 confirmed exoplanets and ~4,000 candidates. Demonstrated that Earth-size planets around Sun-like stars are common (~10% occurrence rate). The mission's photometric precision (20 ppm/hr on a 12th-magnitude star) enabled the first rocky-planet detections. The Kepler Input Catalogue and TESS Input Catalogue together index 1.7 billion stars — the most extensive stellar database ever compiled.

TESS Mission (Ricker et al. 2015, JATIS 1:014003): The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (2018–) surveys the entire sky in 26-sectored 27-day pointings, focusing on bright (V < 12) stars for follow-up characterisation. TESS has discovered 400+ confirmed exoplanets and 6,000+ candidates (TOIs). The mission produces ~200M light curves in its prime survey — each ~30,000 cadences of 2-min or 20-sec integrations — all stored in the MAST archive as Parquet + FITS files.

LIGO GW150914 (Abbott et al. 2016, PRL 116:061102): First direct detection of gravitational waves — the inspiral and merger of two black holes (36 + 29 solar masses) 410 Mpc away. The signal, observed on 14 September 2015, swept through LIGO's two 4-km interferometers (Hanford + Livingston) with a 6.9 ms delay matching light-speed travel between sites. Strain amplitude h ≈ 10⁻²¹ — the most precise length measurement ever made. By the end of O3 (2020), LIGO/Virgo had catalogued 90 GW events (50 BBH, 2 BNS, 1 NSBH, 37 marginal).

LHC Higgs Boson (ATLAS + CMS, 2012, PLB 716:1–29): Discovery of the Higgs boson at m_H ≈ 125 GeV, confirming the Standard Model's last unverified prediction (Higgs 1964). The LHC produces 40 million collisions per second at 13 TeV — only ~1000 events/s survive the trigger. After zero-suppression, ATLAS + CMS together record ~1 PB/year. Jet substructure algorithms (anti-kT, n-subjettiness, JetGNNs) identify boosted H→bb̄, W→qq', top→bqq' — the LHC's biggest machine-learning pipeline.

JWST Early Galaxies (Naidu et al. 2022, ApJL): JWST's CEERS survey revealed galaxy candidates at z ≈ 10–17 (within 300 million years of the Big Bang) — far earlier than Hubble could see. The photometric redshift technique (Lyman-break dropout) plus NIRSpec follow-up spectroscopy confirmed Lyman-α emission. JWST produces ~10 TB/day of raw imaging — Cal pipeline outputs are stored at MAST as Parquet/Arrow tables for community querying. Early-galaxy observations challenge Λ-CDM cosmology — some galaxies appear too massive too soon.
